Go too slowly and the robot wastes time. Go too fast for too long and it slides past. The **stopping law** gives
the fastest speed the robot can have right now and still stop on the goal.

<Scene name="driveToPose" caption="The stopping law at work: the robot holds speed, brakes on the curve, then eases in. Real follower, simulated drivetrain." />

## What it does

[`StoppingLaw`](/reference/follow/stoppinglaw/) works out three speeds and returns the smallest.

```text
speed = min( v_max ,  sqrt(2·a·(d − v·τ)) ,  k·d )
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `d` | distance left to the goal | m |
| `v` | the speed commanded last loop | m/s |
| `a` | braking deceleration | m/s² |
| `τ` | reaction time | s |
| `k` | ease-in slope | m/s per meter, which is 1/s |
| `v_max` | top speed | m/s |

<StoppingCurve />

1. **Top speed.** Far from the goal the law is capped at `v_max`. At the finish of a path that is the path's own
   maximum speed.
2. **The braking curve.** The fastest speed that still stops in distance `d` is `sqrt(2·a·d)`. A drivetrain
   answers late and covers `v·τ` meters first, so the law takes that off. A negative `d − v·τ` counts as zero.

   ```text
   v = sqrt(2·a·(d − v·τ))
   ```

3. **The ease-in line.** Close to the goal the square root is steep, and pose noise becomes a jittery command.
   The straight line `v = k·d` is lower there, so it takes over.
4. **The deadband.** Inside a small band around the goal the law returns zero.

Example: commanded at 3 m/s with 0.5 m to go, the reaction margin is 0.15 m, and the braking curve allows
`sqrt(2 · 12.8 · 0.35)`, about 3.0 m/s. The robot must start slowing now.

## Where it is used

| Method | Used for | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The full law | The last 0.3 m of every path that stops, and across a [rail](/concepts/rail-law/) | As above |
| `StoppingLaw.spin` | Heading. The error in radians replaces `d`, and the result has the sign of the error. | The same, with spin limits |
| `StoppingLaw.brake` | A ceiling on the plan's speed along the whole path, with `d` the distance left | `v = sqrt( v_end² + 2·a·(d − v·τ) )` |
| `StoppingLaw.slowing` | The deceleration being asked for, for the [feed-forward](/concepts/feed-forward/) | `slowing = min( a ,  k·v )` |

- **`brake`** uses the path's end speed as `v_end`. For a path that stops it is zero, so the switch to the finish
  has no seam.
- **`slowing`** returns zero while the law is at its top speed, or while the command is still coming up to the
  law's speed.

## The numbers

The settings live in [`FollowerConfig`](/reference/follow/followerconfig/). These are the library's defaults.

| Setting | Default | Raise it | Lower it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `brakeMetersPerSecondSquared` | NaN, meaning the traction limit | The robot brakes later and harder. | The robot brakes earlier and arrives later. Lower it if the robot overshoots. |
| `latencySeconds` | 0.05 s | A bigger reaction margin: braking starts earlier. | Braking starts later. Too low and the robot overshoots. |
| `easeInPerSecond` | 12 per second | A steeper line: a quicker last few centimeters, more sensitive to pose noise. | A gentler, slower arrival. |
| `headingEaseInPerSecond` | 5 per second | The same, for the last degrees of the turn. | The same. |

| Name | Value | Unit | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `a`, default robot | 12.8 | m/s² | The traction limit |
| Position deadband | 3 | mm | The follower's |
| Heading deadband | 0.3 | ° | The follower's |
| Finish radius | 0.3 | m | Where the full law takes over on a path that stops |
| Crossover, `d = 2a / k²` | about 18 | cm | Where ease-in takes over from braking, ignoring the reaction margin |
| Spin acceleration cap | 720 | °/s² | Replaces `a` in `spin`, on a robot file with no spin limits |
| Spin cap | 540 | °/s | Replaces `v_max` in `spin`, on a robot file with no spin limits |

:::note
In the simulator, a robot braking at the full 12.8 m/s² into a drivetrain that answers in about 70 ms rolled
about 2 cm past the goal and crept back. The defaults suited the simulated drivetrain and were not measured on a
robot. `latencySeconds` and `easeInPerSecond` are the two to tune there. See [Tuning](/guides/tuning/).
:::

## What it does not do

- **It does not know about grip.** The [output limiter](/concepts/output-limiter/) decides how fast the command
  may change.
- **It does not steer around anything.** It gives a speed along a straight line to the goal.
- **It has no memory.** Each call is one formula on the numbers passed in.
