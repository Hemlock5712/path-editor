Under a trench or along a platform edge there is no room to be off-line. A **rail** is a line the robot is held
to while the driver holds a button, and the stick moves it along the line.

:::caution
The rail is a driver-feel feature. Its numbers come from the simulator, and none has been reviewed with a driver
on a robot. Treat every value as a default, not a recommendation.
:::

## What a rail is

| Part | Meaning | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `xMeters`, `yMeters` | Any point on the line | required |
| `directionRadians` | Which way the line runs | required |
| `headingRadians` | A heading to hold | NaN: the driver turns the robot |
| `startMeters`, `endMeters` | Where the rail stops, as distances along it from the point | −∞ and +∞: endless |
| `pullIn` | How fast the rail pulls the robot on, as a share of the driver's speed | +∞: as fast as the robot can |

The rail is in whatever frame the pose is in.

## What it does

[`RailLaw`](/reference/follow/raillaw/) splits the motion into **across** the rail and **along** it.

1. **Across.** The speed toward the line comes from the [stopping law](/concepts/stopping-law/), in one
   dimension. It brakes at `ACROSS_SHARE` of the traction limit `A`.
2. The driver's stick across the rail is not added. On the line, a full stick straight across moves the robot
   0 m/s.
3. **Along.** The speed is the part of the driver's stick velocity that points along the line.
4. While the robot is still getting on, that speed may only change gently. "Getting on" means more than 5 cm off
   the line, or sliding across it faster than 0.05 m/s.

   ```text
   across braking = 0.55 · A
   along change   = 0.85 · sqrt(A² − (0.55 · A)²)
   ```

5. Getting onto the rail has first claim on speed. The along speed gets what is left.

   ```text
   along speed ≤ sqrt(cap² − across speed²)
   ```

   Off the rail, `cap` also leaves out `STEER_MARGIN` of top speed for steering.
6. Spin is counted before driving. The drive speed available is the top speed less the spin's share.
7. [`RailFollower`](/reference/follow/railfollower/) sends the result through the same
   [output limiter](/concepts/output-limiter/) teleop uses.
8. The [feed-forward](/concepts/feed-forward/) is the law's own braking across the rail, and the limiter's step
   along it.

## Soften the pull with pullIn

`rail.withPullIn(share)` caps the pull across at a share of the driver's speed along the rail. The robot then
slides on at a fixed angle.

```text
angle     = atan(pullIn)
joins at  = (how far off) ÷ pullIn, farther along the rail
```

- **The driver's push toward the line counts in full.**
- **Their push away from the line is ignored.**
- **With the stick released the rail still pulls** at up to `SETTLE_METERS_PER_SECOND`.

From the library's rail test, in simulation: a robot 1.2 m off the line with the driver at 3 m/s along it.

| `pullIn` | Peak speed across | Travel turned | Time to the line | Joins after | Driver's speed kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| none | 3.15 m/s | 61° | 0.74 s | 1.9 m | 85 % |
| 1.0 | 3.00 m/s | 48° | 0.72 s | 2.1 m | 97 % |
| 0.5 | 1.50 m/s | 27° | 0.94 s | 2.8 m | 100 % |
| 0.3 | 0.90 m/s | 17° | 1.40 s | 4.2 m | 100 % |
| 0.2 | 0.60 m/s | 11° | 2.02 s | 6.1 m | 100 % |

The `pullIn` share has no recommended value. It is a question of feel for a driver on a robot.

## Ends and heading

| Call | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `rail.between(startMeters, endMeters)` | Gives the rail ends. The stopping law stops the robot at the end ahead. The driver can always drive back. |
| `Rail.through` | Leaves the heading to the driver. Their spin rate passes straight through. |
| `rail.withHeading(radians)` | Locks the heading with the stopping law for heading, the follower's spin limits, `headingEaseInPerSecond` and a 0.3° deadband. |

## The numbers

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ACROSS_SHARE` | 0.55 | The share of the traction limit that braking onto the rail plans on. |
| `ALONG_MARGIN` | 0.85 | The share of the leftover grip the driver gets along the rail while the robot is getting on. |
| `STEER_MARGIN` | 0.1 | The share of top speed kept spare for steering while off the rail. |
| `STEER_BLEND_METERS` | 0.05 m | The steering margin fades out over this distance. Also the "on the rail" distance. |
| `SETTLE_METERS_PER_SECOND` | 0.5 m/s | With a `pullIn` set, the rail still pulls at up to this with the stick released. |
| `ACROSS_SETTLED_METERS_PER_SECOND` | 0.05 m/s | Sliding across the rail slower than this counts as on it. |
| Default robot, `A` | 12.8 m/s² | Gives 7.0 m/s² across and 9.1 m/s² along. |

The reaction time, the ease-in slopes and the spin limits come from the
[`FollowerConfig`](/reference/follow/followerconfig/) the rail was built with.

## What it does not do

- **It does not decide when a rail is on.** Robot code does, usually from a button.
- **It does not avoid obstacles** or know where the walls are.
- **It does not follow a curve.** A rail is a straight line.
- **It does not drive along the rail on its own.** With the stick released the robot gets onto the line and
  stops.

:::note
No driver has driven a rail on a real robot. `ACROSS_SHARE`, `ALONG_MARGIN` and `STEER_MARGIN` were set on a
simulated drivetrain.
:::
