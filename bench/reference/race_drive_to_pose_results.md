# Drive-to-pose race: results

Generated by `python race.py` (about 4 minutes; `--md-only` rebuilds these files from `results.json`; `--quick` is a smoke test).
Plant: the team's `alignsim` swerve plant, 10 noise seeds per cell (each seed draws wheel-radius error 1 % and gyro scale error 0.2 %, steering/gyro/encoder noise, loop jitter). Max speed 4.0 m/s for every method; acceleration 6 m/s^2 for the libraries (ours is limited by AccelerationLimiter, about 9.8 m/s^2 in the sim). Scenario starts are at (0, 0, 0); goals as named.

## Does the plant model slip? Yes.

`plant.py` is a brush/stick-slip tire model (mu static 1.15, kinetic 1.0 by default) at 500 Hz. A wheel that exceeds the friction circle slides, and its encoder then over-reads, so wheel odometry drifts. It exposes time-any-wheel-slipping and the four wheels' slip state; there is no slip distance, so the tables report wheel-slip milliseconds (the four wheels summed, so 4000 ms = all four wheels slipping for one second). Reduced grip = mu 0.70 static / 0.61 kinetic. "Calibrated" = wheel-radius and gyro-scale errors set to zero, so ONLY slip corrupts odometry. Settled = TRUE pose within 2 cm and 2 degrees AND true speed below 5 cm/s, held 0.25 s ("really settled"). Accuracy is always measured against the TRUE plant pose; "odometry error at finish" is |true - believed|. The controllers see wheel odometry plus gyro (no vision) in the main tables; `results_tables.md` also has perfect-pose runs that isolate the control law.

## Summary (accuracy first, then time)

1. **Accuracy against the true pose: the sim does NOT show our Drive To Pose as the most accurate, as shipped.** Its AccelerationLimiter uses MU = 1.0 (9.81 m/s^2), which is right at the plant's friction limit, so every launch spins the wheels (about 1300 wheel-ms) and odometry over-reads. With perfectly calibrated wheels it parks a constant 3.5-4.1 cm short (true error 3.9 cm at 3 m and at 6 m, 3.6 cm on the diagonal, 4.1 cm with the 2 m/s sideways start), "really settled" 0 of 10 runs. CTRE LinearPath re-planned from the measured state and CTRE+P5 finish 0.1-0.3 cm off in the same cases (they accelerate at 6 and barely slip, 10-40 wheel-ms). At reduced grip ours gets much worse (45 cm at 6 m, 34 cm on the diagonal, 54 cm sideways start) because the limiter's MU never adapts; nothing here (ours, CTRE, BLine, PathPlanner) measures or detects slip, they only avoid it by accelerating gently. Lowering our limiter traction to 7 m/s^2 (MU 0.7) removes the problem at normal grip (0.1-0.3 cm calibrated on straights and the diagonal, 1.8 cm on the moving start; settle 1.55 s at 3 m vs 1.50 s) and cuts the low-grip error to 10-22 cm; traction 6 gives 2-5 cm at low grip.
2. **Where the pose is perfect (control law only), ours is excellent**: 0.1 cm final error, no overshoot, fastest settle among the accurate methods in the perfect-pose tables (1.50 s at 3 m, 2.24 s at 6 m, 0.60 s at 0.3 m). CTRE re-plan is as accurate but 0.45-0.65 s slower (2.07 s at 3 m); CTRE setpoint-loop + P5 is 0.25 s slower and overshoots 5 cm; CTRE setpoint FF-only never corrects error (10-13 cm off at 4.5-6 m even with a perfect pose, 8.5 cm on the diagonal, 30 cm with the moving start).
3. **BLine does not beat ours at stopping on a spot with its documented example gains**: kP 5 / 3 / 2 with the default 3 cm end tolerance never settles (final error 7.5 cm at 1 m, about 40 cm at 2-6 m). P on remaining distance cannot brake at 6 m/s^2 from 4 m/s (it needs the speed to fall 5x per second of distance, about 20 m/s^2), so it overshoots 9-69 cm, and it stops the instant it is inside 3 cm, so even a perfect stop is not within 2 cm. Tuned (kP 1.5-4 by distance, end tolerance 1 cm) it is accurate (0.9-4.8 cm true error, the same odometry floor as everyone) but 0.1-1.7 s slower than ours (3 m: 2.04 s vs 1.50 s; 6 m: 3.4 s vs 2.25 s; 1 m: 1.03 vs 0.94 s). BLine DOES beat ours as shipped on the moving start (2 m/s sideways, goal 2 m ahead): 1.6-3.0 cm vs 4.3 cm true error, and at low grip on the diagonal (2.8 vs 34 cm) and moving start (3.4 vs 54 cm), because its gentle low-kP approach barely slips; ours with traction 7 closes most of that gap (2.5 cm and 10-22 cm).
4. **(Follow-up: adding a final hold, the usual team finish, fixes PathPlanner's settling. With a perfect pose it then settles 10/10 in 1.75 s at 3 m, 2.50 s at 6 m, 1.75 s on the diagonal (kP 10 + hold: 1.58 / 2.33 / 1.59 s) vs ours 1.50 / 2.24 / 1.52 s, still overshooting 4.5 cm at kP 5 (1.7 cm at kP 10) where ours overshoots 0; see the follow-up section below.)** PathPlanner (time-sampled trajectory, kP 5, no end settling) ends 4-6 cm past the goal** at every straight distance (3.5-6.1 cm mean true error with the same odometry floor) and never settles at the 2 cm bar; kP 10 cuts it to 1.3-1.8 cm with a perfect pose. It is quick (3 m trajectory 1.25 s to 5 cm, about equal to ours) but fails the moving start badly (75 cm at normal grip, 105 cm at low) because the trajectory assumes the robot already moves along the path and the step in commanded velocity makes the wheels slide for a second or more; through our limiter it also ends 21-57 degrees off in heading on the diagonal.
5. **Same-limiter runs (B in `results_tables.md`, "+ OUR LIMITER" rows)** do not change the ranking: the libraries already accelerate at 6, below the limiter's cap, so the limiter only matters for steps (moving start, heading changes), where it helps CTRE re-plan (moving start 2.2 cm vs 20.4 cm) and hurts PathPlanner's heading on the diagonal. Time: ours is fastest or tied everywhere accuracy-qualified; the only rival that reaches 5 cm sooner is PathPlanner kP 10 at 3 m (1.28 s vs 1.36 s) and it settles in only 5 of 10 runs.

## Matched acceleration: where the remaining time gap comes from (see the "Matched acceleration" section below)

- With everyone at 4 m/s and 6 m/s^2, CTRE setpoint+P5 and PathPlanner reach the mark FIRST (3 m: 1.27 s vs ours 1.39 s) because their trapezoids plan to stop exactly on the goal with no allowance for actuation lag (about 50 ms plus the velocity loop), so they run 2-5 cm past and need a return move; ours brakes on sqrt(2a(d - v*0.05)) with that 50 ms margin and arrives without overshoot, so it really settles first (1.50 s vs 1.58-1.75 s for PathPlanner/CTRE, 2.07 s for CTRE re-plan).
- BLine eases in on P x distance (v = kP*d, tuned kP 2.5), an exponential tail that costs 0.5-0.6 s to get within 2 cm (1.88 s at 3 m) but never overshoots, so its settle gap (2.04 s) is that slow tail alone.
- A hard launch (9.8 m/s^2 for everyone) helps ours and BLine (kP 4-5) by 0.1-0.4 s but the trapezoid methods then overshoot 16-50 cm and slip, so they settle no sooner than at 6 m/s^2; the launch is a small part of ours' lead, the stop is most of it.

## Fidelity caveats

- Ours is ported line for line from `DriveToPose.java` and `AccelerationLimiter.java`; motor model uses the sim's gear ratio (6.75) and wheel radius, not your TunerConstants. The mass (60 kg) and MU (1.0) in the limiter are the file's values (the file says TODO weigh the robot).
- Pose source is wheel odometry plus gyro only. A real robot also fuses vision, which would remove the 3.5-5 cm odometry floor for everybody; with vision the ranking would be set by the "perfect pose" tables (ours and CTRE re-plan on top).
- The libraries were given the same 4.0 m/s and 6 m/s^2 as requested, but ours really accelerates at about 9.8 and brakes at 6. A section in `results_tables.md` re-runs the libraries at 9.8: they then slip like ours (300-1100 wheel-ms) and overshoot 13-20 cm, so the speed gap is not only the law.
- BLine "tuned" rows are the best of a kP x end-tolerance sweep chosen by this sim, not an official recommendation; the README says its gains are examples to be tuned. BLine min-speed and handoff features are not used (single segment).
- PathPlanner's trajectory generator is a re-implementation for one straight segment, not a line-for-line port (see Sources). Slip and traction limits inside PathPlanner's generator (RobotConfig motor and friction model) are NOT modelled; acceleration is capped at 6 m/s^2 only.
- Sim parameters (mu, motor limits, steering lag, 2 ms actuation delay) are marked ASSUMED in `params.py`; none has been fitted to your robot. The mu = 0.70 case is an illustration, not a measurement.
- Ten seeds mostly vary odometry calibration and noise, so the means are tight; "worst" is the maximum of 10, not a percentile.

## Sources and approximations

- **Ours**: `record/v2-code/DriveToPose.java`, `record/v1-code/AccelerationLimiter.java` (this repo, as read). Port is exact except the motor constants noted above.
- **CTRE LinearPath**: Phoenix 6 `wpiapi-java` 26.70.0-alpha-2, `com.ctre.phoenix6.swerve.utility.LinearPath` (sources jar in the Gradle cache). It is only a setpoint generator: two WPILib TrapezoidProfiles (distance along the straight line to the goal, and heading), re-aimed at the goal from the given state every call, with NO feedback inside. CTRE publishes no complete drive-to-pose example I could find, so three usages are run: (a) "re-plan from measured" (current = measured pose and velocity each 20 ms), (b) the Javadoc loop `current = path.calculate(dt, current, goal)` (setpoint feeds itself; output is open-loop setpoint velocity), (c) the same loop plus P 5 on pose error (translation and heading), which is what you must add for feedback. The WPILib TrapezoidProfile used is the sim's port of the 2025 algorithm. Limits: 4 m/s, 6 m/s^2, heading 1 rot/s and 1 rot/s^2 (matched to ours).
- **BLine**: github.com/edanliahovetsky/BLine-Lib branch `wpilib-2027`, commit 4402250 ("2027 beta.3", 2026-10-16): `Follower.execute`, `TranslationGuidance`, `TranslationProgress`, `RotationProgress`, `HolonomicController`, `HolonomicRateLimiter`. Gains from its README quick start (distance 5, heading 3, cross-track 2; the README says these are examples). Default end tolerances 3 cm / 2 degrees, rotation limits 720 deg/s and 1500 deg/s^2 from the BLine-Web project defaults (BLine-Web `projectConfig.ts`; those also list 4.5 m/s and 12 m/s^2, replaced here by 4.0 and 6.0). Approximations: minimum velocity assumed 0 (library default is per-path), path = one segment start pose to goal pose with profiled (linear-in-progress) rotation, command latches to zero when finished as the real command ends.
- **PathPlanner**: github.com/mjansen4857/pathplanner branch `2027`, commit 7fbd798 (2026-10-04): `FollowPathCommand`, `PPHolonomicDriveController` (x, y, theta P controllers plus trajectory feedforward; the 2027 branch's follower is still the time-sampled trajectory with feedback overrides, not a position-based follower). Gains kP 5 translation and 5 rotation (the values in the library's own warm-up command and documentation examples); constraints 4 m/s, 6 m/s^2, 540 deg/s, 720 deg/s^2. `PathPlannerTrajectory` (forward pass, reverse pass, desaturation at 0.05 m resolution, cosine-interpolated heading, trajectory state 0 carrying the robot's actual speed, time-sampled, output zero when the trajectory ends) is re-implemented analytically for one straight path; the RobotConfig motor and friction limits and angular-acceleration limit inside the generator are not modelled.
- Sim: `record/v2-race/sim/alignsim` (read-only copy; controllers in `race.py`).
