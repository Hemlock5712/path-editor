## Simulated drivetrain (`SimDrive`)

Field-relative velocity commands take effect after 20 ms, then the chassis velocity follows them with a
first-order lag (τ = 40 ms), integrated every 1 ms. The robot code reads the true pose and velocity plus
Gaussian noise (σ 2 mm, 0.1°, 0.005 m/s), seeded per run. No slip or motor saturation: the follower's limiter
keeps commands inside the grip budget, and the bench models slip.

The robot code is what a team would write: `FollowerConfig.defaults(robot)` with `latencySeconds` set to this
drivetrain's response delay, 70 ms (latency, lag and half a loop of holding each command), as a team sets it
for its own robot. The robot file the editor saved plans spin at 7.5 rad/s and 10 rad/s², a little under the
follower's own 540°/s and 720°/s², so feedback has room, as the plan's grip (μ 0.7) sits under the limiter's.

## Why the robot and the preview differ where they do

- **Event positions.** A command can only start on a robot loop, and at 4 m/s the robot covers 8 cm between
  loops. The follower fires each event on the loop nearest to passing it, so the error is up to half a loop of
  travel either side; the allowance grows with the planned speed for that reason.
- **Events at a stop.** They fire when the robot gets inside the finish band (2 cm and 1°), which the plan
  itself enters a little before its last instant (about 0.08 s at 6 m/s² braking), so that is when they're due.
- **Settling.** The preview's total ends when the plan does. The follower then finishes with DriveToPose's law:
  slower than 5 cm/s, inside 2 cm and 1°, held for 0.1 s, after taking up any centimetre of overshoot from
  the drivetrain's delay.
